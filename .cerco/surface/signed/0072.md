## Need

What a cell is tinted for was a bare letter repeated in every class that draws, as private char constants: 'f' for the focus in Painter, RowPainter, MenuPainter and DropDownList, 'h' and 'm' in three each, '.' in TextDesktop, TextScreen and AnsiTerminal. DosPalette said what each letter means, as Map.entry('f', ...), and nothing kept the two in step (plan step A3). Tint names the roles once: TintRole is an enum of them, and Tint#paint tints cells of a row in one. What takes a tint now takes a Tint and paints with it: Caption#highlight, EntryRow#put and the frames (Frame, RaisedFrame), so no drawing passes a letter around. Tint#letter() is left only at the boundary of the screen and the palette, where a tint is stored and read as a letter: DosPalette's keys, TextScreen's bare fill, AnsiTerminal reading the bare tint for the desktop pattern, and Painter reading tints() under a shadow. Ctrl+W keeps its own constant: it is a key, not a tint. The letters, and so every test's expected tints, stay as they were. No behaviour changes.

## Considered

`com.fpetrola.tui.Palette#colors(char): String` turns a letter into colours, but takes any char, so it cannot say which letters are tints; `com.fpetrola.tui.Caption#highlight(Screen, int, int, char): void` and `com.fpetrola.tui.EntryRow#put(Caption, char, char): void` paint in a tint they are handed, but as a bare letter, so each caller had to know it.
