## Need

That a frame takes one cell on each side of what it holds was written as loose arithmetic wherever a frame is drawn (plan step B2b): Painter put a window's views at area.column() + 1 and area.row() + 1, Frame drew its lines area.columns() - 2 wide, DropDownList framed its items at width + 2 and items.size() + 2 and wrote them at column + 1, row + 1 + at, and the open menu painted its selected row from start + 1, width - 2. The border is what takes those cells, so it says it: Border#inside(Area) is what a frame drawn around an area holds, Border#around(Area) is the area a frame takes to hold one, and Border#holding(Point, Size) is what a frame placed with its corner at a cell holds, when what is known is the corner and the size held, as for the drop down's list and the open menu. The window's views, the frame's lines, the drop down's items and the open menu's rows, separators and keys now count from what the frame holds. The open menu's width was the frame's: Menu#width() said "its longest item, a blank on each side and the two borders", so whoever placed the box took the borders back off. Menu#width() now means what the menu itself knows, its longest item and a blank on each side, and the frame around it is Border's; the signature is the same, so the change of meaning and doc is said here: TextMenu's AROUND_ITEM goes from 4 to 2, and VisitingViewsTest's open box for Nuevo and Salir is 7 wide, no longer 9. Its only caller, TextPlacedTitle, holds that width with Border#holding, and finds a click across the box with Border#around. No behaviour changes.

## Considered

`com.fpetrola.tui.Border#middle(int): String` draws a row of the frame given how wide its inside is, but each caller still worked out that width, and where the inside starts, on its own; `com.fpetrola.tui.Drawing#drawOn(Screen): void` draws the frame, but tells nothing of where its inside is.
